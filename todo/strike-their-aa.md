# Clear their AA, then strike (his 10-22, 10-23)

His 10-23: *"you're trying to punch through the enemy's air defense... this might mean you attack the air defenses
directly"*; *"if we are constantly failing to strike enemy eco we need to switch to other targets AA can be a good
target. Ideally we just remember what our 'pain points' are. If enemy AA is why our air is useless, attack that AA
with our ground forces, LRPC, or nukes. Once the AA is out of the way, send in the air attack."* His 10-22: *"To
fight against enemy AA we can throw cheap scouts in first."*

The strike planner's targets are economy only (nnair.as NaSnapEnemy, gNaEcoP); AA enters only as a cost, so a base
ringed with AA offers nothing worth a small wing (his game, Cortex seat: the cheapest paying wing was 58 Phoenixes
for 106 net). Pain points exist only as a fade on cells a gunship wing reached and could take nothing at
(NaPainFade), so the wing does not go straight back.

As a price: an AA site is a target worth its own metal plus what it stops protecting -- the next runs' losses it no
longer causes over the cells behind it -- offered to the air, the ground army, LRPC and nukes, with the air waiting
until it falls. Cheap scouts or decoys go in first to draw the AA.
