# A frontline their army dies on

His 11-07: "if you form a frontline strong enough the enemy army will die to it, but that requires considerable
focus on defense"; with "a good [offense] can be a good defense".

Why it matters both ways (his 11-07): it is what humans do against a bonused AI -- the line gives them the damage
efficiency that overcomes the AI's resource bonus. So our AI, which plays humans at a bonus, must not feed its
army into a line (go around it: raids, artillery, air), and can hold one itself where it is the poorer side.

Breaking one (his 11-07, later): a coordinated strike -- nukes, air, tactical missiles, EMP launchers and the
army together -- on the line. Humans hold ONE frontline with nothing behind it; break it and the base is open.
BARb defends in depth, so a test against BARb does not show this payoff. The test bed is ourselves (his 11-07):
once we can build the strong line, self-play pits the line-holder against the line-breaker, and each improves the
other.

What it is: towers, walls and the standing army held together on the line their army must cross, dense enough
that an attack into it trades badly for them. The cost is the defence focus it takes; the balance against
raiding (which pulls their army home instead) is the open question.

Measured context (11-07, 2v2 vs BARb, +0): our army spends 21% of its time on their half against their 31-35% on
ours; our towers' kills are a small share (light towers ~0.8-1.3k metal a game to minute 20).
